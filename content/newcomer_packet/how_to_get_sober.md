## You can recover

If you have a problem with drinking, you may feel like you can't stop. Certainly that's how I felt for years.  I would wake up, promise myself I'd never do that again, then pull into the bar the very same night.

And yet I have stopped.  I haven't taken a drink in 42 years as I write this, and don't plan to.  I live a rich, full life.

## How I did it

I got help.  If you can't stop on your own, all you need to succeed are some motivation and some help. There's nothing wrong with asking for help to stop drinking. Some things are difficult, and we need others to ease the way for us.

The important first step I took was seeing a doctor, who prescribed something to help me detox.  I didn't want to "take drugs" to stop drinking at first, but to stress the seriousness of untreated alcohol withdrawal, she said, "You could die."  Don't risk your life on this. See a doctor, or get to an emergency room, and be honest about how much and how often you drink.

Looking back on it, the other key thing I did was get support right away. As you're stopping drinking, the doctor is there to keep you alive, but to stay stopped, social support is absolutely essential for many people.

In my case, I went to AA as soon as I had the detox prescription in hand, on the first or second day since my last drink.  Nowadays it's even easier to find an AA meeting.

For in-person meetings, your best bet is the meeting guide phone app.

* [Android - Google Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.meetingguide&pli=1)
* [iPhone - App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/meeting-guide/id1042822181#?platform=iphone).

We also have meetings online: https://aa-intergroup.com/meetings

AA worked brilliantly for me for many years, and gave me a great start.  It's free and widely available.  If it works for you, fantastic!  If not, read on.

## What if AA isn't the right fit?

Some people struggle with different aspects of AA.  Atheists and agnostics, especially, may feel unwelcome, given that the program is essentially religious (though AA rebrands it as "spiritual").  As I became increasingly secular over the years, I've tried many secular alternatives, and have put together a list of my favorites in the post [Staying Sober Without Religion, a Collection of Resources](https://www.reddit.com/r/AASecular/comments/1g3dufc/staying_sober_without_religion_a_collection_of/).

Remember, you're doing this to save your life.  If you were sick and couldn't get along with your first doctor, you'd pick another one, wouldn't you?

## What About Therapy Instead?

The combination of professional therapy and abstinence from alcohol also works well, either in combination with group support or on its own.  The downside here is that it is expensive. If you have the money or great insurance, it may be an option for you.  When I was going to school, our student health services provided therapy for free, so I took advantage of it while continuing in AA.

Not everyone who uses AA or another support group can afford or needs therapy, however.

## Conclusion

That's a lot of information to process!  The important elements of success are:

* Believe you can do this!
* Take action to make it happen -- get help!
* Don't be discouraged if it doesn't work the first time you try it. Just try again until you succeed.
* You deserve to get well.
