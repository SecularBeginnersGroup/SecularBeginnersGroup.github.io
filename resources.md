# Secular AA / Secular Sobriety Resources

If you're trying to get sober as an atheist or agnostic [r/SecularAA](https://www.reddit.com/r/AASecular) is here to help you, but many of us found we needed resources beyond Reddit.

## Online Meetings

* [WorldWide Secular Meetings](https://www.worldwidesecularmeetings.com/meetings) - This online meeting list adjusts to your time zone and shows meetings not only for Secular AA, but for other Secular recovery meetings (we're great fans of LifeRing, for example).
* [Secular AA Online Meetings](https://aa-intergroup.org/meetings/?tags=Secular) - Another list focused on Secular AA only.  This may include some meetings that WorldWide Secular Meetings doesn't have yet.


## In-Person Secular AA Meetings

In the AA [Meeting Guide App](https://www.aa.org/meeting-guide-app), select "Filters", then "Community", and check "Secular".  Note that not all meetings meet every day, so you may need to check for meetings by different days of the week.

## Forums on Reddit

* [r/AASecular](https://www.reddit.com/r/AASecular/) - Hey, that's us!  Welcome!  We support atheists and agnostics and all who enjoy a "freethinker" approach to AA. 
* [r/stopdrinking](https://www.reddit.com/r/stopdrinking/) - a support group for sobriety on Reddit! Although I don't think I could have done so myself, some people use this alone to stay sober.



## Other Secular Fellowships

Some other secular approaches to sobriety are:

* [LifeRing](https://lifering.org) -- LifeRing is a fellowship based on the "3 S's:  Sobriety, Secularity, and Self Help." They often use a "How was your week" check in format for their meetings.
* [SMART Recovery](https://smartrecovery.org/) emphasize stigma-free recovery and self-empowererment. Their tools include the Cost/Benefit analysis (based on motivational interviewing), which is helpful in deciding if sobriety is right for you and increasing your willingness to recover.
* [Recovery Dharma](https://recoverydharma.org/) meetings are peer-led, non-theistic, and based on Buddhist principles. Not strictly "secular" since it's derives from one of the world's major religions just as AA does, but for most people in the west, that doesn't matter.

## Traditional AA

This [excellent post]() for making a start in sobriety and AA contains links to AA meetings and resources. Though some in Secular AA are here because they find AA's spirituality uncomfortable, traditional AA has the advantage of many more in person meetings, and some of us use "approaches."

## Literature

[Staying Sober Without God](https://www.amazon.com/Staying-Sober-Without-God-Alcoholism/dp/1733588000) Written by an atheist who struggled at first to get sober in AA, the core of this book are the "Practical 12 Steps", a sane and secular rewrite of the Twelve Steps, together with step-by-step instructions that are often better than what one cand find in the Tweleve Steps and Twelve Traditions book.  Highly recommended.

[Living Sober](https://www.aa.org/living-sober-book) - Contains many tips for staying sober. Although this is traditional conference-approved AA literature, its tips are mostly practical and not "spiritual", so it's sometimes used in Secular AA meetings as a topic leader.  Great for newcomers!

[Beyond Belief](https://www.amazon.com/Beyond-Belief-reflection-nonbelievers-freethinkers/dp/0988115700) - "Agnostic musings" for those who enjoy a daily-reflections-style book.
